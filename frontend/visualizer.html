1. Uniform-Cost vs Heuristic Guidance

Dijkstra computes the lowest exact cost from start g(n). It has no knowledge of where the destination is, expanding outwards in expanding concentric circular waves like water rippling from a pebble.

A* adds a heuristic estimate h(n) to evaluate f(n) = g(n) + h(n). This acts like a gravitational pull toward the destination, drastically reducing visited nodes.

2. Admissibility & Optimality

A heuristic is admissible if it never overestimates the actual cost to reach the goal (h(n) ≤ h*(n)).

Because straight-line (Euclidean) distance is the shortest possible path between two points in physical space, road distance can never be shorter than straight-line distance. This guarantees that A* always finds an optimal shortest path identical to Dijkstra!

3. Real-World Applications

On the Mysuru road network, Dijkstra must explore hundreds or thousands of square city blocks in all directions before settling the destination.

A* prunes irrelevant side streets and dead ends, exploring 70% to 90% fewer nodes while producing the exact same turn-by-turn route. This makes A* the foundational algorithm for GPS and game navigation.
